Users don't read — they scan. Hierarchy controls what they see, in what order, and what they do.

## The Scan Patterns

F-pattern: text-heavy pages (blogs, docs). Z-pattern: landing pages, dashboards. Gutenberg diagram: balanced layouts. Design for the pattern, don't fight it.

## Size & Weight

H1 > H2 > H3 > body > caption. Weight contrast: 700 vs 400 vs 300. Size ratio: modular scale (1.25×). One primary focal point per viewport.

## Colour & Contrast

Accent colour = action (CTAs, links, active states). Muted = secondary (labels, timestamps, disabled). Background = canvas. Never compete: one high-contrast element per section.

## Whitespace as Structure

Macro: section separation (64–128px). Micro: element grouping (8–24px). Law of proximity: related = close, unrelated = space. Whitespace isn't empty — it's active structure.

## Alignment & Grids

12-column grid (desktop), 4-column (mobile). Baseline grid for text rhythm. Optical alignment > mathematical (hanging punctuation, centered icons).

## Directional Cues

Arrows, lines, gaze direction (people looking at CTA), progress bars, numbered steps. Explicit > implicit.

## Mobile Hierarchy

Stack order = priority order. Sticky CTA. Accordion for secondary content. No hover states — tap = action.

## Testing Hierarchy

5-second test: show page 5s, ask "what's this about?" Blur test: blur page, identify focal points. Heatmaps: click/scroll/attention. A/B test: hierarchy variations.

Our cinematic hero: eyebrow (location) → wordmark (brand) → tagline (promise) → description (detail) → CTAs (action). Each step sized, weighted, coloured for scan order.
